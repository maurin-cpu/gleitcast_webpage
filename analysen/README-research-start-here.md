## Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Paraphrasing Quotes
**Wrong**: "User said they check too many apps"
**Right**: "I check 5 different apps every morning and still don't feel confident" — [source]

**Fix**: Always copy exact words. Quotes lose power when paraphrased.

---

### Pitfall 2: Extracting Low-Signal Content
**Wrong**: Extracting "Great flight today!" with no context
**Right**: Skipping generic content, focusing on pain points, workflows, frustrations

**Fix**: Be selective. Not every post is worth extracting.

---

### Pitfall 3: Not Capturing Context
**Wrong**: Quote without noting what prompted it
**Right**: Quote + "Context: Thread asking 'What's your morning routine?'"

**Fix**: Always note what question/situation prompted the comment.

---

### Pitfall 4: Insufficient Segmentation
**Wrong**: Treating all pilots the same
**Right**: Tagging beginner vs. advanced, noting experience level

**Fix**: Always check poster profile for experience clues.

---

### Pitfall 5: Over-Analyzing During Extraction
**Wrong**: Spending 10 minutes debating if a quote is PAIN or WORKFLOW
**Right**: Tag it quickly as both if needed, move on, analyze during synthesis

**Fix**: Extraction is volume game. Analysis comes later.

---

### Pitfall 6: Ignoring Sample Bias
**Wrong**: "Forum users say X, therefore all pilots think X"
**Right**: "Forum users (who tend to be more experienced and opinionated) say X"

**Fix**: Label sources with their bias characteristics.

## When You're Stuck: Troubleshooting Guide

### "I can't find enough beginner quotes"
→ Search YouTube comments on "paragliding weather tutorial" videos
→ Search Reddit for "beginner" + "weather" + "confused"
→ Search Facebook groups for "new pilot" or "anfänger" questions

### "I can't find Swiss-specific content"
→ Search with "Interlaken", "Engelberg", "Föhn", "MeteoSchweiz"
→ Join Swiss Facebook groups (not just international)
→ Search paraglidingforum.com for Swiss city names

### "All quotes sound the same"
→ You might be in an echo chamber - switch sources
→ Look for contradictions (advanced vs beginner perspectives)
→ Search for specific sub-topics (Föhn, airspace, specific tools)

### "I don't understand paragliding terminology"
→ Reference swiss-paragliding-context.md glossary sections
→ Google the term + "paragliding"
→ Ask in forums (or note it as "need to clarify")

### "This is taking too long"
→ You might be over-analyzing during extraction
→ Set a timer: 20 quotes per hour is good pace
→ Skip low-value content faster
→ Focus on 2-3 highest-value sources only

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to speak German/French to do this research?**
A: No. Lots of content is in English. For German/French content, use browser auto-translate or DeepL. The quotes you extract can be translated, just note original language.

**Q: Should I join Facebook groups with my real profile?**
A: Yes, but be transparent. If you plan to later recruit for interviews, mention you're researching for a new tool. Pilots are generally helpful.

**Q: Can I contact people directly to ask questions?**
A: During extraction phase, NO - just observe and extract. After synthesis, YES - reach out for validation interviews.

**Q: How do I know if I have enough quotes?**
A: When you start seeing the same themes repeatedly with no new insights, you've reached saturation. Typically 200-300 quotes is sufficient.

**Q: What if I find contradictory information?**
A: Great! Flag it. Contradictions often reveal segmentation (beginners say X, advanced say Y) or context-dependence. Don't average them out.

**Q: Should I extract from non-Swiss sources?**
A: Yes, but tag them as "non-Swiss" for comparison. General paragliding pains vs. Swiss-specific pains are both valuable.
